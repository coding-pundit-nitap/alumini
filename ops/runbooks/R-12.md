# R-12 — Certificate or domain expiry

**Alerts:** `TlsCertExpiring` (page, < 7 days) · `TlsCertExpiringSoon` (ticket, < 21 days), `ProbeExporterDown` (ticket).

## What it means

- The certificate served at the public URL, as seen by the blackbox probe through Nginx, expires soon. certbot renews at 30 days left, so under 21 days means renewal has failed at least once.
- `ProbeExporterDown`: the blackbox exporter is not answering, so neither the expiry alerts nor `SiteProbeFailing` can fire.
- Domain expiry is not watched by Prometheus. The registrar's auto-renew and its expiry emails cover it (owner checklist).

## Check first

1. `sudo certbot certificates`: the expiry date, and which certificate Nginx uses.
2. `sudo certbot renew --dry-run`: why renewal fails. Usual causes are port 80 blocked, the `/.well-known/acme-challenge/` location missing from the Nginx site, or a DNS change.
3. `journalctl -u certbot.timer -u certbot.service --since -30d`: does the timer run?
4. Has Nginx reloaded since the last renewal? The deploy hook reloads it (README step 3); without a reload it keeps serving the old certificate.

## Mitigate

- `sudo certbot renew`, then `sudo nginx -t && sudo systemctl reload nginx`.
- Check from outside: `echo | openssl s_client -connect <domain>:443 -servername <domain> 2>/dev/null | openssl x509 -noout -enddate`.
- `ProbeExporterDown` → `deploy/monitoring.sh up -d`, then `deploy/monitoring.sh logs blackbox-exporter`.

## Escalate

Under 24 hours to expiry → SEV-1 (reliability §10.5). An expired certificate makes the site unreachable for every browser.

## Resolved when

The probe reports more than 30 days left (`probe_ssl_earliest_cert_expiry`).
